# Rāśi × Nakṣatra — 36 valid combinations across ten areas

**Status:** interpretive research matrix; descriptive reference, not a prediction engine  
**Primary baseline:** `SRC_PVR_INTEGRATED`  
**Cross-checks:** `SRC_BPHS`, `SRC_BPHS_34_45`, `SRC_BRIHAT_JATAKA_1`,
`SRC_RAMAN_HTJH`, `SRC_VASUDEV_MATCHING_CHARTS`

## Scope and reading rule

The apparent 12 × 27 grid has 324 cells, but only **36 combinations are spatially valid**.
A nakṣatra occupies 13°20′ and either lies inside one rāśi or crosses one rāśi boundary.
The tables below cover every valid intersection.

The ten areas are:

1. rāśi degree span;
2. rāśi attributes (element, modality, ruler);
3. nakṣatra attributes (lord, deity, symbol);
4. sign-lord → nakṣatra-lord natural relationship;
5. combined character;
6. principal significations;
7. potential benefits;
8. potential disadvantages;
9. rāśi dṛṣṭi targets;
10. judgment note or necessary modifier.

“Friend,” “neutral,” and “enemy” below are the **directed natural relationship from the
sign lord to the nakṣatra lord**, using the project's PVR-aligned relationship table. A node
is marked separately rather than forcing it into the seven-graha matrix. The relationship is
only one modifying factor; it is not a verdict on the placement.

Rāśi dṛṣṭi belongs to the **sign**, so all three nakṣatra portions within a sign inherit the
same structural targets. Graha dṛṣṭi cannot be assigned from rāśi–nakṣatra alone: it depends
on the actual graha, its occupied sign, and the chart.

## How to use the matrix in an actual chart

Do not interpret a row by itself. Apply the row in this order:

1. identify the occupying graha or Lagna and its exact **pada**;
2. assess the sign lord and nakṣatra lord by house, sign, dignity, strength and avasthā;
3. follow both dispositors and check whether they connect by conjunction, graha dṛṣṭi,
   rāśi dṛṣṭi, exchange or a shared house topic;
4. add the occupied house and the relevant natural/chara kāraka;
5. verify the result in the appropriate varga, especially the pada's D9 navāṃśa;
6. time activation through the relevant daśā and transit layers.

The “benefits” column describes constructive expression under support. The “disadvantages”
column describes failure modes under affliction, weakness or poor integration. Neither column
is inherently promised by the rāśi–nakṣatra pair.

## Source boundaries

- **PVR:** structural reading method, dispositors, natural relationships, rāśi dṛṣṭi and
  the requirement to judge houses, references and influences together.
- **B. V. Raman:** conditional house judgment and the insistence that sign, house, lord,
  occupants, aspects, strength and navāṃśa modify one another. Raman is a methodological
  cross-check here; the concise combination prose is not presented as a verbatim Raman table.
- **BPHS / Bṛhat Jātaka:** classical rāśi and nakṣatra structure, lords and classifications.
- **Gayatri Devi Vasudev:** Gana, Yoni and Nadi values used in the project. These are matching
  taxonomies and should not be inflated into universal personality judgments.
- **Project synthesis:** the combined-character, benefits and disadvantages columns are
  concise deductions from the attributed component fields. They are marked as synthesis and
  should not be quoted as if one classical author supplied this exact 36-row table.
